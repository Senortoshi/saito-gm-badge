Research date: 2026-10-10. Primary sources used: Saito whitepaper v4.0.1 (Dec 2020), Saito Wiki source markdown (github.com/SaitoTech/wiki, pages dated 2025-2026), saito-rust-workspace source constants, saito-lite-rust consensus docs, Saito archive blog posts (2021-2025), the Dec 2025 mainnet press release, and exchange/aggregator pages. ## Key Question 1: Consensus mechanism (routing work, golden tickets, block production, who gets paid)

### Takeaway
Saito replaces PoW/PoS block rewards with "routing work": a transaction's fee is the work it provides, halving at each network hop, and a node may produce a block when its mempool holds enough routing work to meet a falling "burn fee" threshold. All fees in a block are burned at production; they are released only if a miner finds a "golden ticket" (hash puzzle) included in the very next block, and the released fees are split roughly 50/50 between that miner and one routing node selected by a fee-weighted lottery. Block producers are therefore not paid for producing blocks per se; the people paid are fee-collecting routers (especially first-hop/access nodes) and miners.

### Cited Findings
- Routing work: each transaction carries cryptographic routing signatures naming the next hop; the first-hop node earns work equal to the full fee and each later hop earns half the previous hop's amount (10 SAITO fee -> 10, 5, 2.5). — [Saito Wiki consensus.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus.md); same figures in [saito-lite-rust docs/consensus/description.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/description.md)
- "Transactions provide no usable routing work to nodes that are not in their routing path." — [Saito whitepaper v4.0.1 (Dec 24, 2020)](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- Block production is a falling-price "Dutch Clock" auction: the routing work required starts high right after a block and decays toward zero over time ("burn fee curve"); pace of block production is thus set by aggregate network fee flow. — [Saito Wiki consensus.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus.md); [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- The burn fee is recalculated every block from the previous burn fee and elapsed time against a target interval configured as `heartbeat_interval` (no numeric value given on the page). — [Saito Wiki consensus/implementation-notes.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/implementation-notes.md)
- "Nobody is paid when a block is produced. Every fee in the block is burned." Burning makes block production a net cost so attackers always lose money. — [Saito Wiki consensus.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus.md)
- Golden ticket: miners hash on the block hash; a valid solution must be included in the immediately following block or is void. The lottery (a) picks a transaction from the previous block weighted by its share of that block's fees, (b) rehashes to pick a hop from that transaction's routing path weighted by routing work, (c) pays half of the previous block's fees to the ticket finder and half to the selected router. Each half is capped at 1.5x `avg_total_fees`; excess goes to the "graveyard" (permanently burned). — [implementation-notes.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/implementation-notes.md)
- Default "paysplit" = 0.5 (half miners, half routers). — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf); the wiki says the current split is "a fixed near-50/50 with independent caps" and the paysplit vote mechanism "is not live." — [implementation-notes.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/implementation-notes.md)
- Difficulty: +1 if the previous and current blocks both contain golden tickets, -1 (floor 0) if neither does. A consensus floor `MIN_GOLDEN_TICKETS_NUMERATOR / MIN_GOLDEN_TICKETS_DENOMINATOR` requires a minimum ratio of golden-ticket blocks in a rolling window; blocks below it are invalid. — [implementation-notes.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/implementation-notes.md). Source constants: `MIN_GOLDEN_TICKETS_NUMERATOR = 2`, `MIN_GOLDEN_TICKETS_DENOMINATOR = 6` (i.e., at least 2 of 6 recent blocks must carry golden tickets). — [saito-core defs.rs](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-rust-workspace/blob/develop/saito-core/src/core/defs.rs)
- Unsolved blocks: if no golden ticket arrives in time, that block's fees move to the treasury; a portion may later be paid to UTXO slips rebroadcast via ATR; after "several blocks" without a solution, fees go to the graveyard and are burned. — [implementation-notes.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/implementation-notes.md)
- Code confirms treasury/graveyard accounting: `block.treasury = previous_block_treasury + cv.total_payout_treasury - cv.total_payout_atr;` and `block.graveyard = previous_block_graveyard + cv.total_payout_graveyard;` in block.rs; block header size constant `BLOCK_HEADER_SIZE = 389` bytes. — [saito-core block.rs](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-rust-workspace/blob/develop/saito-core/src/core/consensus/block.rs)
- "Block Staking" is listed as a live mainnet feature: "low-overhead and non-inflationary, intended to support security during low transaction volume." — [Mainnet press release, Dec 16, 2025 (GlobeNewswire via Manila Times)](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/12/16/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/saito-blockchain-launches-mainnet-pioneering-decentralised-web3-infrastructure/2244176)
- Staking design (2022 proposal, "Published"): the separate staking table is removed; staking payouts are instead attached to ATR rebroadcast transactions, each receiving a share of the staking treasury proportional to its share of total UTXO value being rebroadcast; the author expects ~25% of all transaction fees to flow back to existing UTXO this way. — [SIP-001 Simplified Staking (May 10, 2022, David Lancashire)](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-implementation-proposals/blob/main/proposals/001_simplified_staking.md). The 2025-2026 wiki confirms "the staking portion of the fee-reward distribution is 25%" and that staking rewards are paid at the same time ATR fees are charged. — [consensus/security-budget-atr.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/security-budget-atr.md)
- Security claims (self-reported): "no 51% attack and no Sybil attack"; "incentive compatible and permissionless"; attackers must match all cumulative routing work and also 100% of mining output to recapture funds; the project calls itself "the only blockchain that is fully secure against 51 percent attacks." — [consensus.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus.md); [attack_vectors.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/attack_vectors.md); [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- Whitepaper states "Patent protection has been secured on these techniques." — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- Whitepaper scalability claim (marketing-grade, unverified): "practical limit for a Saito blockchain today is in the order of 100 TB of data per day." — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because block producers earn nothing directly and all fees are burned at production, the economic actors to court for NFT/app throughput are access nodes (first-hop routers) — they get the largest expected routing payout and have the incentive to run user-facing infrastructure (e.g., an NFT marketplace front end that also serves as a Saito access node).
- The golden-ticket payout cap (1.5x trailing average fees per block) and graveyard burns mean sudden fee spikes (e.g., an NFT mint rush) partly get burned rather than paid out, dampening the incentive to bundle large bursts into single blocks.

### Gaps
- No numeric block time / `heartbeat_interval` was found in any fetched source; the wiki defers to `BurnFee::calculate_burnfee_for_block` in saito-rust.
- No independent (third-party, peer-reviewed, or audit) analysis of the routing-work security claims was found; all security claims trace to project documents.

## Key Question 2: Automatic Transaction Rebroadcasting (ATR) — epochs, fees, what dies, NFT permanence

### Takeaway
Saito is a "circular" or transient ledger: after a fixed "genesis period"/epoch of N blocks, old blocks are pruned and their UTXOs become unspendable unless the block producer re-includes them in a mandatory ATR transaction that embeds the original transaction and deducts a rent fee from the UTXO. The fee is market-derived (design docs say ~2x, the current wiki says "proportional to"/"at least equal to" the trailing average fee-per-byte); UTXOs too small to pay are dropped and their value recycled to the network. ATR rebroadcasts also carry a 25% staking-style payout, so a UTXO worth at least ~4x the fee rate can theoretically persist indefinitely without shrinking. For NFTs this means permanence is pay-as-you-go: an asset survives only while a balance attached to it covers rent each epoch.

### Cited Findings
- Design (whitepaper 2020): nodes delete the oldest blocks at predictable intervals ("epochs"); once a block leaves the current epoch its UTXO "are no longer spendable," but "any UTXO from that set which contains enough tokens to pay a rebroadcasting fee must be re-included in the very next block" via ATR transactions "that include the original transaction data, but have entirely fresh and newly-spendable UTXO." After two epochs producers may delete all block data except the 32-byte header hash. — [whitepaper v4.0.1](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- Fee rule (design): the rebroadcast fee must be "a positive multiple of the average fee paid by new transactions over the previous epoch"; as competition for blockspace raises new-tx fees, ATR fees rise and more old data is pruned, reaching equilibrium where "old data is removed from the chain at the same pace that new data is added." — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- Specific multiplier (2021 deck and lite-rust docs): "ATR transactions pay 2x the average per-byte fee of the new transactions added to the blockchain. The fee is deducted from the new UTXO when it is created." — [Saito 2021 Transaction Rebroadcasting deck (PDF)](https://archive.saito.io/org.saito.tech/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Saito-2021-Transaction-Rebroadcasting.pdf); "twice the smoothed average fee per byte paid by new transactions" — [saito-lite-rust description.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/description.md)
- Current wiki wording (2025-2026) is softer: rebroadcast "requires a balance covering a fee at least equal to the trailing average fee-per-byte. No multiplier is stated." — [implementation-notes.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/implementation-notes.md); "the fee paid at each rebroadcast is proportional to the average fee-per-byte paid during the epoch since the transaction was added." — [security-budget-atr.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/security-budget-atr.md). These conflict on the exact multiplier (2x vs >=1x); treat 2x as the historical design value and the live value as unconfirmed.
- Epoch length: example of N = 100,000 blocks (current epoch at block 500,000 stretches from 400,001). — [description.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/description.md). Source code has `GENESIS_PERIOD` commented out at `100` (test) and `100_000` (non-test), so the live value is set elsewhere (likely config), not confirmed. — [defs.rs](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-rust-workspace/blob/develop/saito-core/src/core/defs.rs). The whitepaper says an extreme design could use "an epoch as short as 24 hours." — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- A block that fails to rebroadcast a qualifying UTXO is invalid; "blocks invalid unless they contain all required ATR transactions." — [implementation-notes.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/implementation-notes.md); [2021 ATR deck](https://archive.saito.io/org.saito.tech/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Saito-2021-Transaction-Rebroadcasting.pdf)
- What happens to data/assets that can't pay: UTXOs without enough balance are simply not rebroadcast and fall off the chain; the 2021 deck lists "dust recycled into ongoing subsidy for block producers" and "zero inflation" as ATR benefits. — [2021 ATR deck](https://archive.saito.io/org.saito.tech/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Saito-2021-Transaction-Rebroadcasting.pdf). The wiki says "removing UTXOs too small to pay their storage burden is part of keeping the chain sustainable." — [security-budget-atr.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/security-budget-atr.md)
- Self-sustaining permanence: ATR rebroadcasts carry a staking payout (25% of fee distribution); "a UTXO that uses its staking rewards to cover rent can persist on-chain indefinitely without its balance shrinking"; to stay in equilibrium a UTXO "should be worth at least four times the fee rate." — [security-budget-atr.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/security-budget-atr.md). The consensus page likewise says "Permanent storage can be self-sustaining if the attached balance keeps covering its own rebroadcast costs." — [consensus.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus.md)
- Whitepaper rationale: nodes that do not store the whole chain "are incapable of producing new blocks, as they do not know which payments must be rebroadcast" — this is how ATR forces full-data retention within the epoch. — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- "Pruning" is a mainnet-readiness benchmark the team says it met: "successful ATR pruning cycles in production." — [Dec 2025 press release](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/12/16/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/saito-blockchain-launches-mainnet-pioneering-decentralised-web3-infrastructure/2244176)
- Operational history: the first ATR cycle completed in April 2024 and the third in April 2025, with the project paying "5% APY" to native-chain wallets every six months as "ATR Rewards." — [First ATR Cycle and Rewards (Apr 12, 2024)](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/first-atr-cycle-and-rewards/); [Third ATR Reward Cycle (Apr 10, 2025)](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/third-atr-reward-cycle/). Note: a six-month cycle is the observed wall-clock cadence of these reward posts, not a documented block count.
- "Token persistence threshold": as of Apr 2025 the minimum native balance guaranteed to persist was 35,000 SAITO, with the threshold scheduled to reach 0 ("full token persistence") before end-2025; the wiki roadmap (published Nov 2025) says token persistence is now "a reality." — [Third ATR Reward Cycle](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/third-atr-reward-cycle/); [wiki roadmap.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/roadmap.md)
- A Nov 2024 post said the team was "internally considering an 'ATR Hyperloop' which would significantly shorten the epoch for ATR" to get more production data per unit time. No adoption confirmation found. — [Saito Summer 12 – The Big Picture (Nov 4, 2024)](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/saito-summer-12-the-big-picture/)
- Known economic edge case from SIP-001: "Dead Man's Wallet" — profitable but unspendable slips could accumulate growing payouts forever; Richard Parris suggested slightly increasing their size each loop so they eventually become a cost drain. — [SIP-001](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-implementation-proposals/blob/main/proposals/001_simplified_staking.md)

### Inferences
- For an NFT, "permanence" on Saito is conditional: the asset's bound UTXO must retain enough SAITO to pay rent each epoch (rent scales with the transaction's byte size times trailing fee-per-byte). Large embedded media raises per-epoch rent proportionally, so a heavy on-chain image NFT needs a correspondingly large attached balance to survive indefinitely, while a tiny pointer-only NFT is cheap to keep alive.
- The 4x-fee-rate equilibrium rule implies an issuer can "endow" an NFT with a balance that makes it self-perpetuating via the 25% staking payout, but that endowment is itself exposed to fee-market volatility: if network fees rise faster than the payout, the endowment erodes and the asset eventually drops off.
- Because the full original transaction is embedded inside each ATR rebroadcast, rebroadcast cost is proportional to the whole transaction's size, not just the slip; data-heavy transactions get progressively more expensive to keep relative to their token value.

### Gaps
- The live mainnet epoch length (in blocks or days) and live fee multiplier were not found in any dated 2025-2026 source; the "six months" ATR reward cadence is the only observed cycle period.
- No fetched source documents exactly how a non-fee-paying data transaction (e.g., pure data with zero-value outputs) is treated at epoch end beyond "falls off."

## Key Question 3: On-chain data storage — size limits, arbitrary data, cost

### Takeaway
Saito transactions can carry arbitrary message data (the apps — chat, RedSquare posts, games — all work by putting JSON/application payloads in transactions), and NFTs can embed CSS, JavaScript, images, and programs. Pricing is per byte via the fee market, and persistence is per byte via ATR rent. No hard block or transaction byte cap was found in the documents or constants fetched; the whitepaper frames capacity as bounded by network hardware rather than a protocol constant, and the 2021 ATR deck says a blocksize cap "can" exist but is not required.

### Cited Findings
- NFTs "can combine css, javascript, images, embedded-programs and much more," built from the browser wallet "without smart contracts." Recipes include replacing a card image with "an image embedded in the NFT" vs. an external image, CSS theme NFTs, and JSON-type NFTs combining JS and CSS. Programmatic NFTs must be "enabled" by the recipient wallet after receipt. — [wiki nfts.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/nfts.md)
- Fees are measured per byte: the consensus values track "smoothed avg fee per byte (last epoch)"; ATR fee is based on average fee-per-byte. — [block.rs](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-rust-workspace/blob/develop/saito-core/src/core/consensus/block.rs); [security-budget-atr.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/security-budget-atr.md)
- "the network can still have a blocksize cap -- the market mechanism still works. In this case ATR simply creates a maximum theoretical size for the blockchain." — [2021 ATR deck](https://archive.saito.io/org.saito.tech/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Saito-2021-Transaction-Rebroadcasting.pdf)
- ATR "works best when blockspace is unconstrained and priced by the market." — [security-budget-atr.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/security-budget-atr.md)
- Whitepaper: "nothing technically stopping us from building a blockchain that is as decentralized and open as the public Internet backbone"; the limit is economic, not a protocol constant; claims ~100 TB/day practical limit (unverified). — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- Applications "connect to blockchain access points to send and receive data"; apps like RedSquare, Arcade, Chat run in-browser on transaction data. — [saito-lite-rust README](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust); [saito.io](https://saito.io/)
- Historical block data retention: producers must keep full data within the current epoch (to build ATR txs) and may delete block bodies after two epochs, keeping only the 32-byte header hash. — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf); [description.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/description.md)
- Default consensus values in code: `total_fees: 5000`, `burnfee: 1`, `difficulty: 1` (nolan units; 1 SAITO = 100,000,000 nolan). — [block.rs](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-rust-workspace/blob/develop/saito-core/src/core/consensus/block.rs); [defs.rs](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-rust-workspace/blob/develop/saito-core/src/core/defs.rs)
- Press release framing: "Smart NFTs: dynamic, logic-bearing assets meant to provide on-chain functionality without a complex virtual machine." — [Dec 2025 press release](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/12/16/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/saito-blockchain-launches-mainnet-pioneering-decentralised-web3-infrastructure/2244176)

### Inferences
- Since fees and rent are per byte and there is no documented per-tx cap, the practical constraint on embedding an image in an NFT is cost over time (rent every epoch), not a hard size ceiling. An "external image" option in the official NFT recipes signals the project itself expects a mix of on-chain and off-chain media.
- An NFT whose media is embedded in the originating transaction pays rent on that media forever; one referencing external storage pays rent only on the small pointer but inherits the off-chain link-rot risk familiar from Ethereum NFTs.

### Gaps
- No explicit max block size or max transaction size constant was found in defs.rs, block.rs (first ~380 lines), the README, or the wiki; the node config template (which may set limits) returned 404. A code search of `blockchain.rs`/`mempool.rs` would be needed.
- No published fee schedule (SAITO per KB) or live average fee-per-byte figure was found; the saito.io explorer page rendered empty.

## Key Question 4: State of the network 2025-2026 — mainnet, node software, throughput, upgrades

### Takeaway
Saito declared mainnet on Dec 16, 2025 (self-reported: >70M blocks processed, "no known exploits," ATR pruning cycles run in production). The codebase was consolidated in 2026: `saito-lite-rust` was archived (Sep 4, 2026) in favor of a `saito` monorepo (Rust core + Node/JS + WASM), and a "Refactor 2026" release (networking, consensus engine, UI, native assets/NFTs, RustScript, Saito Store) reached RC1 with a target date of Aug 3, 2026. No reliable node-count, TPS, or block-time figures were found.

## Key Question 5: Token economics — supply, inflation, fee distribution, wrapped vs native, migration

### Takeaway
SAITO exists as (a) a native layer-1 UTXO token and (b) wrapped ERC-20 (0xFa14...74B57B) and BEP-20 (0x3c6d...134da) tokens issued in 2021 for exchange trading. Code and wiki put max supply at 7 billion; aggregators disagree (CoinGecko 3B; CMC 1.97B circulating / 4.2B total / 7B max). The network has zero block-reward inflation; all payouts come from fees (50/50 miner/router on solved blocks, ~25% of fees recycled to UTXO holders via ATR/staking). Migration from wrapped to native is official (saito.io/migration), optional, one-way, and was promoted in Aug 2025 with a 5-7% bonus.

## Key Question 6: Saito modules and application-layer architecture (in-browser nodes, Red Square, Arcade, how apps store state)

### Takeaway
Saito apps are JavaScript "modules" that run inside the browser wallet (a lite client with WASM consensus code), look like websites, and communicate by sending fee-paying transactions through access nodes; app state is whatever the module reads from on-chain transactions plus local wallet options, with "archive" nodes/modules and Vault for off-chain-but-key-controlled storage. Refactor 2026 moves ownership of tokens/NFTs into consensus ("shared consensus hashmap"), adds P2SH scripts and RustScript, and makes a marketplace (Saito Store) a network-level service.

### Cited Findings
- "Saito-Lite is in-browser software that follows Saito consensus and runs applications in the browser... apps run inside the browser, look and feel like websites, and connect to blockchain access points to send and receive data." Modules live in `/mods` and are enabled via `/config/modules.config.js` (`core` and `lite` sections); build with `npm run compile dev` to `/web/saito/saito.js`; default port 12101. — [saito-lite-rust README](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust)
- Module inventory (archived lite client): arcade, archive, atr, chat, crypto, explorer, mixin, redsquare, registry, relay, videocall, stun, encrypt, fileshare, library, migration, recovery, settings, dozens of games (chess, poker, settlers, twilight, etc.), tutorial01-07, zk-poll; no `nft` directory in the visible listing. — [saito-lite-rust /mods](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/tree/master/mods)
- Homepage app suite (2026): RedSquare (social/publishing), Arcade (games), Chat (encrypted), Saito Talk (browser-to-browser calls), Stack (publishing), Vault (key-controlled storage), Store (commerce). "Modules are browser-native and get access to network, wallet, identity, peer-service, and transaction capabilities." Asset types: passes, subscriptions, apps, extensions, tokens, certificates; "a single wallet handles shared ownership across apps." — [saito.io](https://saito.io/)
- Wallet crypto modules: third-party chains (Polkadot, Kusama, and via Mixin 500+ assets) can be added to the wallet; a wiki NFT recipe adds NEAR support "to the browser wallet, where it can be used with Arcade games." — [saito-lite docs/applications.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite/blob/master/docs/applications.md); [wiki nfts.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/nfts.md)
- NFT data model (Nov 2024): "A Saito NFT is a special UTXO that is bound to another UTXO that has fees and can be transferred normally"; the bound UTXO travels with the standard transaction; "split NFTs" (fungible tokens on the same framework) were "under development and testing" on branch feature/814/nft. — [Saito Summer 12 (Nov 4, 2024)](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/saito-summer-12-the-big-picture/)
- Refactor 2026 assets: wallet-native NFT creation; "new slip types support programmable ownership"; ownership "tracked by consensus within a shared consensus hashmap"; NFT splitting/merging and recombination; multisig, conditional, time-locked, escrow-capable ownership; "P2SH-protected NFTs"; "programmable royalty enforcement"; "applications inherit asset support rather than implementing their own token standards." — [wiki refactor-2026/assets.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/refactor-2026/assets.md)
- Refactor 2026 overview: users can "create tokens and NFTs, send them like ordinary payments, split or merge them, and lock them into programmable scripts" supporting "multisig and enforced royalties"; RustScript lets developers write scripts controlling who can spend assets, access protected content, claim rewards, or gain membership benefits; scripts appear to users as objects such as "Access Keys." — [wiki refactor-2026.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/refactor-2026.md)
- Saito Store: sellers list an item and can go offline; listed assets are secured with P2SH; "the network completes the sale when a buyer arrives" via atomic execution, no server custody; sells software, game assets, subscriptions, CSS themes, access keys, "any NFT asset"; open API on listing pages; interoperable with third-party stores. — [wiki refactor-2026/saito-store.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/refactor-2026/saito-store.md)
- Programmatic NFTs (JS/CSS) must be explicitly "enabled" by the receiving wallet; an example NFT "stores a click count in the wallet's options" (i.e., local wallet state, not chain state). — [wiki nfts.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/nfts.md)
- Earlier (2021) roadmap concepts: Services Module (server-side functionality discoverable via standard API), Peers API, splitting the NPM core into blockchain/wallet/helpers/modules packages, and an advertising revenue model so most users can use Saito for free. — [saito-lite-rust docs/roadmap.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/roadmap.md)
- Lancashire quote on dev model: apps built "with web-native tools, without Solidity, proprietary stacks, or centralized backends." — [Dec 2025 press release](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/12/16/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/saito-blockchain-launches-mainnet-pioneering-decentralised-web3-infrastructure/2244176)

### Inferences
- NFT ownership on Saito is consensus-level (a UTXO/slip), not a smart-contract mapping; transfer = spending the bound UTXO. This makes NFTs cheap to send but ties their lifetime to the ATR rent of the bound fee-carrying UTXO.
- Because app modules execute arbitrary JS/CSS delivered as NFTs inside the wallet, "programmatic NFTs" are effectively code plugins; the explicit "enable" step is the security boundary.
- Long-lived app state beyond the epoch (e.g., full post histories in RedSquare) must rely on archive nodes or re-posting; the on-chain record itself ages out unless rebroadcast.

### Gaps
- No technical spec of the new slip types, NFT ID / shard ID scheme, or RustScript syntax was found (assets.md is a feature list). No live marketplace volume or NFT count was found.

## Key Question 7: Known criticisms, limitations, security considerations

### Takeaway
No independent security audit, peer-reviewed analysis, or substantive third-party critique of Saito's routing-work consensus was found; all security claims ("no 51% attack," "no Sybil attack") are self-published. Project documents themselves admit limits (eclipse/sole-access-point attacks, block-flooding at edge nodes, dead-man's-wallet payouts), the project is small by every public metric, and the transient ledger makes "permanence" of NFTs and app data an ongoing cost rather than a one-time guarantee.

### Cited Findings
- Admitted limitation: "All consensus systems fail in situations where one's view of the longest chain is dictated by an attacker... Saito is no different than other blockchains in this regard." — [attack_vectors.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/attack_vectors.md)
- Admitted limitation: nodes "may thus forward the first block they receive from attackers," and edge nodes "may offer attackers an access point for data-flooding attacks." — [attack_vectors.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/attack_vectors.md)
- Sybil defense is economic/evolutionary, not cryptographic: nodes "that fail to detect sybils would earn less and be pushed out by competition" — "a design argument, not a measured result." — [attack_vectors.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/attack_vectors.md)
- Death-of-Moore's-Law section concedes attackers could avoid losses if they control 100% of hashpower and can match 100% of stake. — [attack_vectors.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust/blob/master/docs/consensus/attack_vectors.md)
- The whitepaper's security result depends on "assumptions about the rationality of the network" / "bounded rationality" for the (unimplemented) dynamic paysplit, and the advanced powsplit/paysplit mechanisms are described as theoretical or "not live." — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf); [implementation-notes.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/consensus/implementation-notes.md)
- Patents: "Patent protection has been secured on these techniques." (Potential licensing/forking constraint; no patent numbers given.) — [whitepaper](https://saito.io/saito-whitepaper.pdf)
- Economic edge case: "Dead Man's Wallet" — unspendable but rent-covering slips accrue growing ATR/staking payouts. — [SIP-001](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-implementation-proposals/blob/main/proposals/001_simplified_staking.md)
- Bridge risk history: Anyswap/Multichain ERC20-BEP20 bridge became unreliable; manual ERC-20 withdrawals; repeated scam warnings about fake migration helpers. — [Token Persistence Update 2023](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/token-persistence-update-september-4-2023/); [BSC to Mainnet 2024](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/binance-smart-chain-to-mainnet/); [wiki tokenomics.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/tokenomics.md)
- Network resets: the chain was periodically hard-forked/reset with regenerated issuance files (2023), and small balances below the persistence threshold (35,000 SAITO as of Apr 2025) were not guaranteed to survive until "full token persistence" late 2025. — [Token Persistence Update 2023](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/token-persistence-update-september-4-2023/); [Third ATR Reward Cycle](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/third-atr-reward-cycle/)
- Independent-source search outcome: web searches for critiques, audits, Reddit discussions, or academic reviews of "routing work" returned only project materials, promotional reposts (CoinStats, Substack), and unrelated papers; "I found no peer-reviewed analysis, audit, or independent critique of Saito's routing-based incentives or its 51% resistance claim." — [search summary; CoinStats repost](https://coinstats.app/news/m9X3ZXV20Q_Understand-Saitos-Proof-of-Routing-Work-Consensus-Mechanism-as-Well-as-Some-Criticism-on-Conventional-Blockchains); [Substack advocacy post](https://mukdde.substack.com/p/the-economic-solution-to-the-blockchainweb3)
- Scale/adoption signals: market cap ~$0.7-1.1M; core Rust repo 4 stars; archived lite client with 351 open issues; the 2024 call for volunteers to revive the Realms NFT game. — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/saito); [saito-rust-workspace](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-rust-workspace); [saito-lite-rust](https://github.com/SaitoTech/saito-lite-rust); [Saito Summer 12](https://archive.saito.io/saito.tech/saito-summer-12-the-big-picture/)
- Programmatic NFTs execute JS/CSS inside the user's wallet after an "enable" step — a code-execution surface. — [wiki nfts.md](https://github.com/SaitoTech/wiki/blob/master/nfts.md)

### Inferences
- For an NFT project, the dominant practical risks are (1) economic: rent-based permanence means an unfunded or under-endowed NFT silently expires at epoch end; (2) liquidity/adoption: a sub-$2M-cap, low-activity network with one-way migration; (3) protocol churn: hard-fork upgrades (Refactor 2026 changes slip types and asset serialization) could require asset migration; (4) wallet-side code execution for JS NFTs.
- Counterbalancing advantages: no VM gas model, per-byte market pricing, consensus-native ownership with P2SH scripts/royalties, and a built-in custody-free marketplace are genuinely differentiated versus EVM NFT stacks — but these are project claims with no third-party validation found.

### Gaps
- No third-party audit, formal verification, or academic evaluation of Saito consensus or ATR was found.
- No incident history (exploits, chain halts) beyond the project's own "no known exploits" claim; no uptime data from an independent monitor.

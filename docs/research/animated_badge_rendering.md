# Animated badge rendering: how platforms and browser engines keep many small animations cheap

Research date: 2026-10-11. Scope: engineering techniques for rendering dozens of small animated badges/avatars/emotes in a long scrolling feed, applied to an SVG hexagon badge whose metallic rim is a rotating `<linearGradient>` driven by SMIL `animateTransform` on `gradientTransform`.

Sourcing note: roughly 20 searches/fetches were run. Several primary pages could not be fetched (Discord Help Center returns HTTP 403 to fetchers; Twitch Help is a JS-only Salesforce page; the Mozilla support thread blocked loading). Where a claim rests only on a search-engine summary of an official page, or on a well-known MDN URL that was not re-fetched in this session, that is flagged inline.

## Key Question 1: Discord — animated avatars, decorations, profile effects, emoji: formats and throttling

### Takeaway
Discord's publicly documented strategy is (a) rasterized formats for most animated assets (GIF/APNG, plus Lottie only for verified/partnered stickers), (b) *context-gated playback*: animated avatars are static in chat/member lists and only animate on hover or when the profile is opened, and (c) a global "Reduced Motion" switch that freezes GIF avatars and animated emoji everywhere except the full profile view. No Discord engineering blog post describing the rendering pipeline (canvas vs. Lottie, concurrency limits) was found.

### Cited Findings
- Discord's official sticker format enum is PNG=1, APNG=2, LOTTIE=3, GIF=4; uploads are limited to PNG, APNG, GIF, or Lottie JSON of up to 512 KiB; animated stickers are capped at 5 seconds and 320x320 px; Lottie stickers can only be uploaded on guilds with the VERIFIED and/or PARTNERED feature — [Discord Developer Docs: Sticker](https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/sticker)
- Discord blocks upload of Lottie files that contain raster images (community feedback post, i.e. Discord restricts Lottie to pure-vector content) — [Discord support community post](https://support.discord.com/hc/ja/community/posts/4403611337367-Allow-Lottie-Stickers-to-use-raster-images)
- Discord's official Reduced Motion article (as summarized by the search engine; the page itself returned 403 to the fetcher) says Reduced Motion "disables GIF avatars from playing in the chat unless you click into the user profile" and that "Animated Emojis will now be static in the Emoji Picker, within chat, statuses, and reactions", and that Discord "will reduce animations and movements within the app regardless of your system configurations" — [Discord Help: Reduced Motion (article 360040613412)](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040613412)
- Users report that enabling Reduced Motion force-disables the separate "Automatically play GIFs when possible" and "Play animated emoji" toggles, and ask for them to be independent — [Discord support community post](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/1500000383582-Allow-Automatically-play-GIFS-when-possible); related request — [Make autoplaying gifs separate from reduced motion setting](https://support.discord.com/hc/da/community/posts/360051182913-Make-autoplaying-gifs-separate-from-reduced-motion-setting)
- Third-party guide (not official) describes animated avatar behavior: the GIF "plays on hover in the member list and auto-plays in the full profile view" — [itslinuxfoss guide](https://itslinuxfoss.com/how-set-up-animated-avatar-discord-nitro/)
- Discord describes Profile Effects as "unique and snazzy animated effects that play on your profile when someone views your profile" (search-engine summary of the official article; article returned 403) — [Discord Help: Profile Effects](https://support.discord.com/hc/articles/17828465914263)
- Discord's product blog on avatar decorations covers ownership/Shop, not rendering — [Discord blog: Avatar Decorations](https://discord.com/blog/avatar-decorations-collect-and-keep-the-newest-styles)
- Mobile: a community post says Discord mobile has no GIF autoplay option, only an option that makes animated stickers animate on long press (user report, possibly dated) — [Discord support community post](https://support.discord.com/hc/vi/community/posts/360052910753-Setting-to-turn-off-auto-play-on-GIFs-on-mobile-devices-accessibility-setting)
- Discord's image libraries did not support animated WebP at the time of a long-running community thread (user-sourced, dated) — [Discord support community: Add support for WEBP Animated Images and Emojis](https://support.discord.com/hc/th/community/posts/360034605072-Add-support-for-WEBP-Animated-Images-and-Emojis?page=3)

### Inferences
- The dominant Discord technique for feeds is not making animation cheaper but *gating when it plays*: static frame by default in dense lists, animation on hover / focus / profile open, and a single user-level kill switch. A badge next to usernames in a feed is exactly the "member list" case Discord freezes.
- Discord's decision to keep Lottie behind a verified/partnered gate and ban raster-in-Lottie suggests Lottie decode/render cost and abuse risk were concerns; raster formats (GIF/APNG) are the mass-market path.

### Gaps
- No Discord engineering blog post about avatar/decoration/profile-effect rendering (format, renderer, concurrency caps, off-screen pausing) was found; discord.com/blog/engineering did not surface anything relevant in search.
- Exact current in-app toggle names and whether avatar decorations are APNG, Lottie, or video could not be confirmed from a primary source.

## Key Question 2: Twitch animated emotes and badges

### Takeaway
Twitch animated emotes are square GIFs with hard limits (≤60 frames, ≤1 MB auto-resize or ≤512 KB each at 28/56/112 px, no more than three flashes per second). Those caps are themselves a throttling technique: per-asset frame and size budgets bound decode and memory cost in chat. No official statement on chat-level playback throttling or a native viewer "disable animated emotes" setting was found.

### Cited Findings
- Animated emotes: square GIF; cannot exceed 60 frames; auto-resize mode takes one GIF 112x112 to 4096x4096 px up to 1 MB; manual mode uses 28x28, 56x56, 112x112 GIFs each ≤512 KB; must not flash or flicker more than three times within one second; the first GIF frame is the default static thumbnail (vendor summaries of Twitch's rules; Twitch's help page could not be fetched) — [Elgato: Emotes guidelines](https://developer.elgato.com/en/graphics/emotes); [makeemoji: GIF vs WebP for emotes](https://cf-production.makeemoji.com/blog/gif-vs-webp-for-discord-slack-twitch-emotes)
- One vendor page lists a minimum of 100 ms per frame (unconfirmed against Twitch) — [socialcal emote resizer](https://www.socialcal.app/twitch-emote-resizer)
- Twitch's official page was not retrievable (Salesforce "CSS Error / Loading" shell) — [Twitch Help: Animated Emotes](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/animated-emotes)

### Inferences
- A "static first frame as thumbnail" rule is the raster analogue of what we need for the SVG rim: a cheap still state that is identical to frame 0 of the animation, so swapping between animated and static is visually seamless.

### Gaps
- No primary Twitch source on whether chat pauses emotes when the tab is hidden or limits simultaneous animated emotes; no evidence of animated WebP support in Twitch chat.
- Native Twitch viewer setting to disable animated emotes: not found (third-party extensions like BTTV/7TV offer this, but no Twitch doc was located).

## Key Question 5: Telegram (Lottie/TGS, rlottie), Reddit, WhatsApp

### Takeaway
Telegram constrains vector animation at the *authoring* stage (512x512 canvas, 60 fps, ≤3 s, ≤64 KB, long list of banned After Effects features) and renders with rlottie into caller-supplied bitmaps, with clients caching rendered frames on disk (users observe hundreds of MB of frame cache). Reddit and WhatsApp yielded no primary engineering sources in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Telegram .TGS requirements: 512x512 canvas, 60 FPS, max 3 seconds, max 64 KB after Bodymovin render, Lottie-based, must loop, objects must not leave the canvas; banned features include Auto-bezier keys, Expressions, Masks, Layer Effects, Images, Solids, Texts, 3D Layers, Merge Paths, Star Shapes, Gradient Strokes, Repeaters, Time Stretching, Time Remapping, Auto-Oriented Layers; "less than 30 KB in size – six times smaller than the average photo" — [Telegram: Animated Stickers](https://core.telegram.org/animated_stickers)
- python-lottie author says the official unsupported-feature list is inaccurate (masks, mattes, star shapes, gradient strokes, repeaters, solids actually work; layer effects, images, skew, text, animated layer transforms do not), and that TGS is gzip-compressed Lottie JSON — [python-lottie on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/lottie/)
- rlottie renders a given frame into a caller-provided buffer (`rlottie::Surface surface(buffer, width, height, stride)`), synchronously via `renderSync(frameNo, surface)` or asynchronously via `render(frameNo, surface)` returning a `std::future`; `loadFromData` takes a `cacheKey`; the project is archived and no longer maintained — [Samsung/rlottie README](https://github.com/Samsung/rlottie)
- Telegram bug tracker: users report the animated-sticker cache reaching ~200 MB after login and ~750 MB in total, and hypothesize each frame is stored as a separate file (user reports, not Telegram statements) — [Telegram bugs tracker c/13523](https://bugs.telegram.org/c/13523/16)
- A 269-byte malformed sticker can saturate memory and crash the app (renderer robustness concern) — [pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/15404/telegram-269-byte-sticker-crash)
- tlottie (Rust/WASM) claims 23–73% faster frame times than rlottie/thorvg (project's own benchmark, unverified) — [tlottie README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/tlottie@0.1.24/README.md)

### Inferences
- Telegram's two big moves are: cap the *source* complexity so every sticker is cheap, and pre-render frames to bitmaps that are cached and reused (trading disk for CPU). The browser-side equivalent is pre-rendering the vector rim to an image sequence or to a shared canvas once per frame and blitting it N times.

### Gaps
- No Telegram engineering document on frame-cache design was found (only user bug reports).
- Reddit (avatars/awards) and WhatsApp (animated stickers) produced no primary engineering sources in this pass; not covered.

## Key Question 6: Browser engine facts — compositor vs main thread, SMIL, pausing, visibility, containment

### Takeaway
Only `transform` and `opacity` animations can run on the compositor; anything that changes paint (gradients, `gradientTransform`, custom-property-driven `conic-gradient`) re-rasterizes on the main thread every frame. SMIL is still supported in all engines (Chrome's 2015 deprecation was suspended in 2016) and can be paused per `<svg>` root with `pauseAnimations()`, which is Baseline. `content-visibility: auto` skips rendering off-screen subtrees entirely, and `update: slow` / `prefers-reduced-motion` are the standard media hooks.

### Cited Findings
- "CSS-based animations, and Web Animations where supported natively, are typically handled on a thread known as the 'compositor thread'. This is different from the browser's 'main thread'"; "Where you can, you should avoid animating properties that trigger layout or paint"; "If any animation triggers paint, layout, or both, the 'main thread' will be required to do work" — [web.dev: Why are some animations slow?](https://web.dev/articles/animations-overview)
- Opacity is cheap "As long as this property is in its own layer, changes to it can be handled by the GPU during the compositing step"; Chromium and WebKit create a layer for elements with an opacity transition/animation; "each layer uses memory. On devices with limited memory creating new layers may cause more of a performance problem than the one you are trying to solve" — [web.dev: Why are some animations slow?](https://web.dev/articles/animations-overview)
- "Where possible, restrict animations to `opacity` and `transform` to keep animations on the compositing stage of the rendering path"; `will-change`: "Because layer creation can cause other performance issues, we don't recommend using it early in your optimization process"; apply with JS shortly before a change and "remove the property when the change has stopped"; "Use the `will-change` property sparingly, and only if you encounter a performance issue"; use Chrome DevTools Rendering > Paint Flashing / Firefox Toggle paint flashing to find unexpected repaints (last updated 2020-10-06) — [web.dev: How to create high-performance CSS animations](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)
- `transform` and `opacity` "can be heavily optimized whether the animation is driven by JavaScript or CSS" — [web.dev: Animations and performance](https://web.dev/articles/animations-and-performance)
- `SVGSVGElement.pauseAnimations()` "suspends (i.e., pauses) all currently running animations that are defined within the SVG document fragment corresponding to this `<svg>` element, causing the animation clock corresponding to this document fragment to stand still until it is unpaused"; Baseline Widely available since July 2015; counterparts `unpauseAnimations()` and `animationsPaused()`; spec is SVG Animations Level 2 — [MDN: pauseAnimations](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SVGSVGElement/pauseAnimations); per-browser: Chrome 4+, Firefox 2+, Safari 3.1+, Edge 12+ — [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_svgsvgelement_pauseanimations)
- Chrome filed "Intent to deprecate: SMIL" on blink-dev in 2015 (in favor of CSS Animations / Web Animations) — [blink-dev thread](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/5o0yiO440LM/m/YGEJBsjUAwAJ?pli=1); the deprecation was suspended in 2016 after pushback and SMIL still ships in all major browsers — [SVG Backgrounds (Sep 2022)](https://www.svgbackgrounds.com/?p=2496); [ZKM: SMIL browser support](https://extinction.zkm.de/events/smil-browser-support-collapse)
- A WebKit bug requests hardware acceleration for `animateTransform` "similar to what CSS transforms already received", noting repaint was unreliable — [WebKit bug 53089](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=53089); an older WebKit bug on SVG gradient/mask performance found much of the time in `SVGResourceMasker::applyMask()` — [WebKit bug 19118](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=19118)
- Firefox users report stuttering with many simultaneous `animateTransform`/`animate` elements that Chrome handles smoothly; the responder advised reducing the number of animated elements or moving to CSS (search-engine summary; page blocked direct fetch) — [Mozilla Support question 1555224](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/questions/1555224)
- SVG 2 draft lists `animateTransform` targeting `patternTransform` as an SVG extension; `gradientTransform` is not called out, so support may vary — [SVG 2 WD 2015 animate chapter](https://www.w3.org/TR/2015/WD-SVG2-20150409/animate.html)
- `content-visibility: auto` lets the browser "skip rendering work (layout and painting) for an element until it's needed"; it applies layout, style and paint containment, and off-screen elements also get size containment and "stop painting and hit-testing their contents"; demo showed 232 ms → 30 ms (7x) initial render; needs `contain-intrinsic-size` (the `auto` keyword remembers the last rendered size); content stays in the DOM/accessibility tree; Chrome 85, Edge 85, Firefox 125, Safari 18; Baseline Newly available 2025-09-15 — [web.dev: content-visibility](https://web.dev/articles/content-visibility)
- `@media (update: slow)`: device "can't render or display changes fast enough to be perceived as smooth animation", e.g. e-readers or severely underpowered devices; `fast` means "regularly updating features such as CSS Animations can be used"; MDN example wraps the `animation` declaration in `@media (update: fast)`; Baseline Widely available since September 2023 — [MDN: update media feature](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/update)
- iOS Low Power Mode caps `requestAnimationFrame` at 30 fps by design (WebKit r213169; regressed to 90 fps in r244182, fixed in r261113 by running the rendering-update refresh monitor at 30 fps) — [WebKit bug 215745](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=215745); cross-origin iframes also get rAF throttled until user interaction (2018) — [Popmotion blog](https://popmotion.io/blog/20180104-when-ios-throttles-requestanimationframe); no way to override — [GreenSock forum](https://greensock.com/forums/topic/26826-any-way-to-keep-gsap-running-at-60fps-while-mobile-device-is-in-low-power-mode/)
- Sharing gradient `<defs>` across inline SVGs: WebKit bug — when the defining SVG sits inside a `display: none` parent, a gradient referenced via `url()` from another SVG does not render (stroke renders, gradient fill does not); `visibility: hidden` parent renders correctly; Firefox fine in both — [WebKit bug 243341](https://auto-bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=243341); sprite workaround: hide the defs SVG with zero size + absolute positioning + `visibility: hidden` instead of `display: none` — [sprite.sh issue](https://lightrun.com/answers/edenspiekermann-sprite-sh-gradients-not-visible); Safari historically broke fragment-only `url(#id)` when a `<base href>` is present (resolved) — [WebKit bug 189499](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=189499)
- Animated CSS gradient border technique: register the angle with `@property` so it interpolates, then animate the `conic-gradient` start angle; `@property` is best supported in Chromium, with Firefox 128+ and Safari 16.4+ — [learnwithjason: Animated CSS gradient borders](https://learnwithjason.dev/blog/animated-css-gradient-border); [web.dev: CSS border animations](https://web.dev/articles/css-border-animations?hl=ja)
- Lottie web: canvas renderer is generally recommended for "complex animations or many instances"; SVG renderer is slower with complex animations; guidance to keep each animation under ~1000 DOM elements; canvas can be moved to a worker via `transferControlToOffscreen` while SVG cannot (qualitative, no benchmarks) — [Hackernoon: developer puts Lottie under the microscope](https://hackernoon.com/developer-puts-lottie-under-the-microscope); [SVGator: Lottie vs SVG](https://www.svgator.com/help/getting-started/lottie-vs-svg)
- Pre-rendered animated image formats: animated AVIF ≈ 3–10% of equivalent GIF size — [cococonvert](https://cococonvert.com/blog/gif-vs-webp-vs-apng); lossy animated WebP 64% smaller than GIF, lossless 19% smaller (Google figures as cited) — [instawp](https://instawp.com/?p=2433); WebP decodes faster than AVIF — [SpeedVitals](https://speedvitals.com/blog/webp-vs-avif/); APNG sizes inconsistent (one test found every APNG larger than the source GIF) — [Cloud Four: animated GIF alternative](https://cloudfour.com/thinks/animated-gif-alternative)
- Not re-fetched this session but standard MDN references for the mechanisms below: `prefers-reduced-motion` — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion); `animation-play-state` — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/animation-play-state); Page Visibility API (`document.hidden`, `visibilitychange`) — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Page_Visibility_API); Intersection Observer — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Intersection_Observer_API); `will-change` — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/will-change); `NetworkInformation.saveData` — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/NetworkInformation/saveData); `navigator.hardwareConcurrency` — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/hardwareConcurrency)

### Inferences
- A SMIL `animateTransform` on `gradientTransform` changes a *paint server*, not an element transform. By the compositor rule above, this must re-rasterize the stroked hexagon (and anything in the same layer) on the main thread every frame, for every badge. With N badges that is N rasterizations per frame; it cannot be promoted to the compositor the way a `transform` on an element can. (Inference from web.dev's paint/compositor model plus the WebKit bug showing SMIL transforms lack acceleration; no document directly states "gradientTransform SMIL forces repaint".)
- The same applies to a CSS `@property`-driven `conic-gradient` angle: the gradient image is regenerated per frame on the main thread; it is cheaper to author but not cheaper to render than SMIL. The compositor-friendly rewrite is to rotate an *element* (a gradient-filled disc clipped/masked to a ring) with `transform: rotate()`, so the gradient is rasterized once and only the texture transform changes per frame.
- `pauseAnimations()` is the right primitive for SMIL because it freezes the document fragment's clock (frame stays wherever it was) and is supported everywhere; pair it with `IntersectionObserver` for off-screen and `visibilitychange` for hidden tabs.
- `content-visibility: auto` on feed items is complementary: skipped subtrees are not painted at all, so even an un-paused SMIL badge off-screen costs nothing to paint (it may still tick its timeline; pausing avoids that too). Its 2025 Baseline status means it is safe to ship with graceful fallback.
- No hard "GPU layer count limit" number exists in sources; the only documented constraint is memory per layer. Treat "one compositor layer per badge" (e.g. via `will-change: transform` on 50 badges) as a cost to be measured, not free.

### Gaps
- No primary Chromium/WebKit/Gecko document was found stating explicitly which thread SMIL runs on; the compositor statements above are about CSS/Web Animations. Treat "SMIL is main-thread paint" as a strong inference consistent with the WebKit bug, not a quoted fact.
- No benchmark numbers comparing Lottie vs CSS vs `<video>`/APNG per instance were found; only qualitative guidance.
- Safari-specific SMIL quirks (beyond `display:none` defs) were not verified in this pass.

## Key Question 7: Mobile considerations

### Takeaway
Mobile browsers already throttle for you (iOS Low Power Mode → 30 fps rAF; cross-origin iframes throttled), so the badge should degrade gracefully: respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, `update: slow`, `saveData`, and `hardwareConcurrency`, and never fight the OS throttle.

### Cited Findings
- iOS Low Power Mode caps rAF at 30 fps by design — [WebKit bug 215745](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=215745); no override exists — [GreenSock forum](https://greensock.com/forums/topic/26826-any-way-to-keep-gsap-running-at-60fps-while-mobile-device-is-in-low-power-mode/)
- `update: slow` is meant for "severely underpowered devices" and is Baseline since Sept 2023 — [MDN: update](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/update)
- web.dev warns that extra compositor layers can hurt more than help "on devices with limited memory" — [web.dev: Why are some animations slow?](https://web.dev/articles/animations-overview)
- Discord's Reduced Motion applies "regardless of your system configurations" (app-level override in addition to OS setting) — [Discord Help 360040613412](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040613412)

### Inferences
- Offer an in-app "animate badges" toggle (default on, off when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`), mirroring Discord's model, and also auto-disable when `(update: slow)` matches or `navigator.connection.saveData` is true.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies battery impact of N small SVG animations; `hardwareConcurrency` thresholds are a heuristic without published guidance.

## Recommendations for our SVG rim (ranked by payoff / effort)

Context: a hexagon stroke painted with a `<linearGradient>` whose `gradientTransform` is rotated by SMIL `animateTransform`, appearing up to dozens of times per screen next to usernames. Goal: keep the animation visible.

1. **Play-on-demand gating (Discord/Steam model), static otherwise.** Render the badge static by default in the feed and start the rotation only for badges that are (a) in the viewport and (b) in a small "active" set — e.g. hovered/focused, the post currently in view, or at most K (say 6–8) badges nearest the viewport center. Engine reason: cost is linear in the number of badges repainting per frame; the cheapest animation is one not running. Discord freezes GIF avatars in chat/member lists and animates on hover/profile ([Discord Help 360040613412](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040613412)); Steam mini-profile backgrounds play only on hover ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/pointsshopitems)). Make the static state identical to frame 0 (Twitch's "first frame is the thumbnail" rule) so the swap is seamless. Caveat: K-active-badges logic needs the same IntersectionObserver you add in #3.

2. **Move the rotation from `gradientTransform` to an element `transform`.** Restructure the rim as: a `<g>` (or a `<circle>`/`<rect>` sized to cover the hexagon) filled with the metallic gradient, rotated with a CSS `@keyframes { to { transform: rotate(360deg) } }` on that element, and the hexagon *ring* applied as a `clip-path`/`mask` (a hexagon ring path, i.e. outer hexagon minus inner hexagon, or a hexagon path with a thick stroke used as a mask). Engine reason: `transform` is one of the two properties that can run on the compositor thread; animating a paint server (`gradientTransform`) or a gradient angle must re-rasterize every frame on the main thread ([web.dev animations-overview](https://web.dev/articles/animations-overview); [web.dev animations-guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). Visually it is the same effect (a rotating sheen); the gradient is drawn once and only the texture is rotated. Caveats: whether an SVG child element with a CSS transform gets its own compositor layer varies by engine (Chromium and Safari composite CSS transforms on SVG elements in modern versions; Firefox has lagged — not verified in this pass, so profile with Paint Flashing). If you prefer to keep inline SVG minimal, do the same with a plain HTML `<div>` holding a `conic-gradient`/`linear-gradient` background, rotated via `transform`, and masked with `mask-image` (an inline SVG hexagon ring as the mask), with a static SVG hexagon on top. Do not animate the `conic-gradient` angle via `@property` — that is main-thread paint every frame, the same cost class as SMIL ([learnwithjason](https://learnwithjason.dev/blog/animated-css-gradient-border) shows the technique; cost is an inference from the compositor rule).

3. **Pause off-screen and in hidden tabs.** If you stay on SMIL: one `IntersectionObserver` for all badges; call `svg.pauseAnimations()` when not intersecting and `unpauseAnimations()` when intersecting; on `visibilitychange` with `document.hidden`, pause all. `pauseAnimations()` freezes the fragment's clock and is Baseline across Chrome/Firefox/Safari/Edge ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SVGSVGElement/pauseAnimations); [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_svgsvgelement_pauseanimations)). If you move to CSS (#2), use `animation-play-state: paused` via a class instead. Engine reason: a paused timeline does no style recalc/paint. Caveat: Firefox has user-reported stutter when many SMIL elements animate at once ([Mozilla Support 1555224](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/questions/1555224)), which is exactly what pausing off-screen mitigates.

4. **`content-visibility: auto` + `contain-intrinsic-size: auto <h>` on each feed item.** The browser skips layout and paint for off-screen items entirely, including any badge inside; Baseline since 2025-09-15 (Chrome 85 / Firefox 125 / Safari 18) ([web.dev content-visibility](https://web.dev/articles/content-visibility)). Engine reason: skipped subtrees are not painted or hit-tested. Caveats: avoid DOM APIs that force layout of skipped subtrees; still pair with #3 because a skipped subtree's SMIL timeline may still tick (not verified; cheap to also pause).

5. **Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, `update: slow`, `saveData`, and expose an app toggle.** `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` → no rotation (static sheen at a fixed angle); `@media (update: slow)` → same ([MDN update](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/update)); in JS also check `navigator.connection?.saveData` and a low `navigator.hardwareConcurrency` (heuristic). Mirror Discord's app-level "Reduced Motion" that applies regardless of OS setting ([Discord Help 360040613412](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040613412)). Engine reason: zero cost when disabled; accessibility compliance. Caveat: with SMIL, a media query cannot stop the timeline directly; you need `pauseAnimations()` or remove the `<animateTransform>` element. With CSS (#2) the media query alone suffices.

6. **One shared gradient definition instead of one per badge.** Put a single `<svg width="0" height="0" style="position:absolute">` with `<defs><linearGradient id="rim-metal">…</linearGradient></defs>` once in the page and reference `fill="url(#rim-metal)"` from every badge. Engine reason: one paint-server object instead of dozens, fewer DOM nodes, and (if you keep SMIL) one `<animateTransform>` ticking instead of N, all badges share the same phase. Caveats: do not hide the defs SVG with `display: none` — WebKit then fails to render the referenced gradient; use zero size / `position:absolute` / `visibility:hidden` instead ([WebKit bug 243341](https://auto-bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=243341); [sprite.sh workaround](https://lightrun.com/answers/edenspiekermann-sprite-sh-gradients-not-visible)); Safari historically broke `url(#id)` when a `<base href>` is present ([WebKit bug 189499](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=189499)). Note this reduces DOM/tick cost but *not* per-badge rasterization: each badge still repaints when the shared gradient changes, so combine with #2 or #3.

7. **Budget the animation itself.** Lower the effective frame rate and complexity: use a longer `dur` (slow rotation reads as "premium" and halves perceived cost per visual change is nil, but repaints still occur per frame), reduce gradient stops, avoid filters (`feGaussianBlur`, drop shadows, masks) on the animated element — WebKit found SVG mask application to dominate paint time ([WebKit bug 19118](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=19118)), and web.dev notes blur-like effects paint far slower than flat fills ([web.dev animations-guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). Telegram's approach is the same idea at authoring time: hard caps on canvas, fps, duration, size, and banned expensive features ([Telegram animated stickers](https://core.telegram.org/animated_stickers)). Caveat: with SMIL or CSS, frame rate is not directly settable; `steps()` timing in CSS can cut the number of distinct frames (e.g. `steps(30)` over the loop) which reduces how often a visually different frame must be painted.

8. **Pre-render to a looping raster (APNG/animated WebP/AVIF) as the "many instances" fallback.** For the dense feed, ship the rim as a small looping animated image (Steam uses 224x224 APNG for avatar frames, <2 MB, looping, with a still PNG fallback — [Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/pointsshopitems); Twitch uses GIF ≤60 frames — [Elgato summary](https://developer.elgato.com/en/graphics/emotes)). Engine reason: the browser's image decoder handles frames, typically with frame caching, and identical `<img src>` URLs share one decoded animation; no SVG paint tree per instance. Format guidance: animated WebP is ~64% smaller than GIF (lossy) and decodes faster than AVIF; AVIF is smallest (3–10% of GIF) but slower to decode; APNG preserves alpha and is widely supported but sizes vary ([instawp](https://instawp.com/?p=2433); [SpeedVitals](https://speedvitals.com/blog/webp-vs-avif/); [cococonvert](https://cococonvert.com/blog/gif-vs-webp-vs-apng); [Cloud Four](https://cloudfour.com/thinks/animated-gif-alternative)). Caveats: raster scales poorly to DPR (ship 1x/2x/3x); you lose crispness and the ability to recolor; browsers do not expose a per-image pause API (you must swap to the still image to "pause"); verify Safari handles your chosen format at your DPR.

9. **Single shared render loop onto one canvas sprite, blitted per badge (Telegram/rlottie model).** Render the rotating rim once per frame to an offscreen `<canvas>` (or pre-render the loop's 30–60 frames into a sprite sheet once at load), then draw it into each visible badge's canvas via `drawImage`, or display via `background-image` with `background-position` stepping. rlottie's design — render a frame into a caller-supplied buffer, async, with a cache key — is this pattern ([rlottie README](https://github.com/Samsung/rlottie)); Lottie guidance prefers canvas for "many instances" and allows `OffscreenCanvas` in a worker ([Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/developer-puts-lottie-under-the-microscope)). Engine reason: one rasterization per frame regardless of N; blits are cheap GPU texture copies. Caveats: more code; canvases are not vector-crisp without DPR scaling; keep one rAF loop and stop it when no badge is visible or the tab is hidden; on iOS Low Power Mode rAF is capped at 30 fps anyway ([WebKit bug 215745](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=215745)), so design the loop to look fine at 30 fps.

10. **Cap concurrent animated badges per screen.** Independent of technique, enforce a maximum (e.g. 8–12) of simultaneously animating badges; others show the static frame until they enter the "active" set (scroll position, hover). No platform publishes its number, but Discord's and Steam's hover-only rules are the extreme case (K≈1). Engine reason: bounds worst-case per-frame work on long feeds with many badge holders.

11. **Avoid blanket `will-change` / layer promotion.** Do not add `will-change: transform` to every badge; each layer costs GPU memory and can hurt on low-memory devices ([web.dev animations-guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide); [web.dev animations-overview](https://web.dev/articles/animations-overview)). Only the currently animating element (from #1/#10) should be promoted, and only while animating.

12. **Measure before and after.** Use Chrome DevTools Rendering > Paint Flashing and Firefox's Toggle paint flashing to confirm that after #2 the badges no longer flash on every frame, and the Performance panel's layer view to confirm you did not create dozens of layers ([web.dev animations-guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). If SMIL is retained, test Firefox specifically with 30+ badges, given the user-reported stutter with many SMIL elements ([Mozilla Support 1555224](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/questions/1555224)).

Suggested combination for the feed: #2 (CSS transform on a masked gradient element) + #3 (pause off-screen/hidden) + #4 (`content-visibility`) + #5 (reduced motion / app toggle) + #10 (cap concurrent). Keep SMIL only if you need the exact existing markup, in which case #1 + #3 + #6 are the minimum. Reserve #8/#9 for the case where measurement shows dozens of simultaneously animating badges are a hard requirement.
